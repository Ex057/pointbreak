PART 1 - CANDLESTICK ANATOMY
================================================================================
Every candle tells a four-part story: Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC).

- REAL BODY: the distance between the open and the close. A white/green body
  means buyers won the session (close above open). A black/red body means
  sellers won (close below open).
- UPPER SHADOW (wick): the distance from the top of the body to the session
  high. It shows where buyers tried to push price but failed to hold.
- LOWER SHADOW: the distance from the bottom of the body to the session low.
  It shows where sellers pushed price down but were rejected.

Key interpretation principle from the book: candles are a visual record of the
BATTLE between buyers (bulls) and sellers (bears). Long bodies = conviction.
Long shadows = rejection. Tiny bodies = indecision. Everything else is a
combination of these three ideas.

PART 2 - SINGLE-CANDLE PATTERNS (BOOK SUMMARY)
================================================================================

1. DOJI
   - Open and close are virtually equal, producing a cross shape.
   - Meaning: perfect equilibrium; neither side is winning.
   - Context matters: after a long uptrend, the bulls' streak of victories is
     suddenly in doubt. After a long downtrend, it hints the bulls are finally
     finding the courage to buy and the tide may turn.
   - Book's example stock: INET (Doji Star near a bottom, followed by a rally).

2. LONG-LEGGED DOJI
   - A more dramatic doji: price traveled far intraday (usually a big upper
     shadow) before profit-taking dragged it back.
   - A close below the candle's midpoint signals notable weakness.
   - Book's example: K (Kingsmen), where it appeared near a swing high.

3. GRAVESTONE DOJI
   - Open, close, and low are all near the same level; a long upper shadow
     remains. Price rallied hard intraday, could not hold altitude, and
     collapsed back to the open by the close.
   - The book calls it "probably the most ominous candle of all" - it is a
     classic topping signal.

4. DRAGONFLY DOJI
   - The mirror image: price opened, sold off hard, and buyers drove it all
     the way back to the open. Long lower shadow, no body.
   - Fairly infrequent; when it appears it often resolves bullishly,
     PROVIDED the stock is not already overbought (the book suggests checking
     Bollinger Bands and stochastics before trusting it).
   - Book's example: DSGT at a swing low.

5. HAMMER
   - Appears after a prolonged DOWNTREND. Strong selling early in the session,
     but the market recovers to close near or above the open - it is
     "hammering out" a bottom.
   - Validity conditions per the book:
     a) A definite downtrend must precede it.
     b) Lower shadow at least 2x the size of the body.
     c) The next day should show continued BUYING (confirmation).
     d) No (or tiny) upper shadow; a white body is slightly stronger.
   - Book's example: KCE.

6. HANGING MAN (the "hangman")
   - Identical shape to the hammer but appears after an extended UPTREND.
     Looks like a hanging figure with legs swinging beneath.
   - It forms because dip-buyers rush in during an intraday sell-off, but the
     very existence of that sell-off is the warning.
   - Validity conditions: definite uptrend; lower shadow 2x the body;
     continued SELLING the next day; little to no upper shadow. A black body
     is slightly more bearish.
   - Book's example: MC.

7. INVERTED HAMMER
   - Occurs only after a sustained downtrend (stock likely oversold).
   - Long upper shadow, small body at the bottom. Trapped longs sell into any
     strength, which is why confirmation the next day matters.
   - Book's example: STEC.

8. SHOOTING STAR
   - The bearish twin of the inverted hammer; appears only at a potential
     market TOP, ideally after a gap higher.
   - The market rallies sharply intraday (longs feel in full control), then
     profit-taking erases it; the close returns near the open, leaving a
     small body with a large upper shadow and little/no lower shadow.
   - Validity: uptrend; upper shadow 2x body; continued selling next day.
   - Book's example: WICE.

9. MARUBOZU
   - Japanese for "close-cropped": a long candle with no upper or lower
     shadow (or nearly none).
   - White marubozu = extreme buying conviction; black marubozu = sellers
     eager to flee.
   - The book stresses watching the NEXT day: if there is follow-through, the
     stock tends to trend in that direction for several sessions.
   - Book's example: INTUCH.

10. SPINNING TOP / HIGH WAVE
    - Spinning top: small real body, small overall range. Combined with low
      volume it signals trader disinterest / indecision.
    - High Wave: small body but with LONG shadows on both sides - an active
      tug of war with no winner. On high volume, it reveals general confusion
      about direction.
    - Practical use per the book: technical analysts see these and "join the
      sidelines," waiting for confirmation before committing.
    - Book's examples: SET INDEX (spinning tops), SF (high wave + engulfing).

PART 3 - TWO-CANDLE PATTERNS (BOOK SUMMARY)
================================================================================

1. BULLISH ENGULFING
   - After a significant downtrend, a white candle opens below the prior
     close and closes above the prior black candle's open - its body swallows
     the previous body (shadows need not be engulfed).
   - Book's example: MINT.

2. BEARISH ENGULFING
   - The mirror image after a significant uptrend: a large black body
     swallows the prior white body.
   - Book's example: ECL.

3. DARK CLOUD COVER
   - After an uptrend, a black candle opens ABOVE the previous day's high and
     then closes more than halfway into the prior white body.
   - The deeper the penetration, the more powerful (approaching a bearish
     engulfing). Extra weight if it occurs at a resistance area on strong
     end-of-day volume.
   - Book's example: SCN.

4. PIERCING PATTERN
   - The bullish mirror: after a downtrend (often a 5-15 day mini-trend), a
     white candle opens below the prior day's LOW and closes more than
     halfway into the prior black body.
   - Book's example: SET INDEX.

5. BULLISH HARAMI
   - A large black body followed by a smaller white body contained within it
     ("harami" = pregnant in Japanese; the small candle is the baby).
   - Implies the downtrend's momentum has stalled; stock poised to move up.
   - Shadows can be any size in theory, but in practice they stay inside the
     prior body.
   - Book's example: CPF.

6. BEARISH HARAMI
   - After an uptrend: a large white body followed by a small dark body
     inside it. Book's example: BBL (shown with a harami cross + EMA10,
     Force Index and Directional Movement confirmation - note how the book
     itself combines candles with indicators).

7. TWEEZERS (TOP / BOTTOM)
   - Two nearby sessions share (almost) the same HIGH (tweezers top) or the
     same LOW (tweezers bottom).
   - The match can be body-to-body, shadow-to-shadow, or shadow-to-body; the
     point is the market rejected the same price twice.
   - Book's example: BH.

8. HOMING PIGEON
   - Downtrend; long black candle, then a SMALLER black candle whose body is
     fully inside the first body (like a harami where both candles are
     black).
   - Confirmation: a following candle closing below the pattern's lowest low
     CANCELS it; a strong up day confirms the reversal. Shorts covering
     usually lifts volume above the recent norm.
   - Book's example: IRPC.

9. MATCHING LOW
   - Two black candles closing at exactly the same low after a downtrend.
     The market's failure to close below that level twice tells the bears a
     support floor has been hit.

10. MEETING LINES
    - Bullish: long black candle, then a long white candle that closes at
      (more or less) the SAME level as the prior close.
    - Bearish: the mirror after an uptrend.
    - Statistics quoted by the book: bullish version ~56% up resolution;
      bearish ~49/51 - i.e., these are weak signals needing confirmation.

11. ON NECK / IN NECK / THRUSTING LINES (a family of weak bounces in a
    downtrend - all usually CONTINUATION bearish)
    - ON NECK: after a long black candle, the next day gaps down but bounces
      only to the previous day's LOW. Weak bounce = sellers resume.
    - IN NECK: bounce reaches the previous CLOSE or a hair above it. Some
      short covering, but no trend change.
    - THRUSTING: bounce closes near but slightly BELOW the midpoint of the
      prior black body. Stronger than On/In Neck, but still weaker than a
      true Piercing Line (which closes ABOVE the midpoint).
    - The psychology: each failed bounce that cannot reclaim meaningful
      ground emboldens sellers to press again.

12. SEPARATING LINES (continuation)
    - Bullish: in an uptrend, a black candle is followed by a white candle
      that OPENS at the same level as the black candle's open and rallies.
      Book stats: ~72% resolve up.
    - Bearish version mirrors it in a downtrend (~63% resolve down).

13. ABOVE / BELOW THE STOMACH
    - Reversal signals where the second candle opens at or beyond the
      midpoint of the first candle's real body. Modest reliability
      (roughly 40-66% depending on variant per the book's rankings).

14. KICKER
    - One of the most explosive patterns. Bullish version: stock is falling,
      last red candle closes at the bottom of its range, then the next day
      price GAPS OPEN above the previous day's high and close. This "shock
      event" forces shorts to cover and drags in new longs. Reversed for the
      bearish version.
    - Book's example: MINT.

PART 4 - THREE-CANDLE AND MULTI-CANDLE PATTERNS (BOOK SUMMARY)
================================================================================

1. MORNING STAR (bullish, 3 candles)
   - Day 1: large dark candle in a downtrend.
   - Day 2: small-bodied "star" (indecision) - the book notes a perfect star
     gaps away, but small shadows are tolerated.
   - Day 3: large white candle recovering at least halfway into Day 1's body,
     ideally on strong volume.
   - Book's example: VIBHA.

2. EVENING STAR (bearish, 3 candles)
   - Day 1: long white candle in an uptrend (bulls in full control).
   - Day 2: a star that GAPS HIGHER (black or white, small body, often a
     large upper shadow).
   - Day 3: black candle retreating substantially into Day 1's body. The
     deeper the retreat, the more powerful. A gap between Day 2 and Day 3
     strengthens it further but is rare in equities.
   - Book's example: TRUE.

3. THREE BLACK CROWS (bearish)
   - Three consecutive long black candles "sitting like crows in a tall
     tree." Most predictive when the first crow closes below the prior
     candle's real body.
   - Each day OPENS higher than the prior close (looks like recovery) but
     CLOSES at a new low - sellers regain control every session.
   - Infrequent, but swing traders should be very alert when it appears.
   - Book's example: LPN.

4. THREE WHITE SOLDIERS (bullish)
   - Most potent after an extended decline plus sideways consolidation.
   - Three long white candles; each opens within (ideally the upper half of)
     the prior day's range and closes near its high with little/no upper
     shadow.
   - The first soldier is itself a reversal candle ending the downtrend.
   - Book's example: BEM.

5. STICK SANDWICH
   - Bullish version (in a downtrend): black candle (new low), white candle
     opening above its close, then a black candle that opens above the white
     candle's close but closes back at the FIRST candle's closing level -
     sandwiching the white candle. Book stats: resolves down 62% / up 38% -
     i.e., treat with caution and demand confirmation.
   - Bearish version mirrors this in an uptrend.

6. TRI-STAR DOJI
   - Three consecutive dojis in a downtrend, the middle one dipping below
     the other two. Extreme indecision at a potential exhaustion point.
     Book stats: ~52% up vs 48% down - weak alone, needs confirmation.

7. ABANDONED BABY (bullish)
   - Downtrend; long black candle; a DOJI that gaps DOWN (abandoned below);
     then a long white candle opening above the doji.
   - One of the strongest reversal signals (book's efficiency rank A-,
     ~70% up resolution). Confirmation is optional but recommended.

8. UNIQUE THREE RIVER BOTTOM
   - Resembles a morning star. Day 1: black candle engulfing what follows.
     Day 2: small black body that opens higher, trades at a NEW LOW (long
     lower shadow), then closes near its high. Day 3: short white candle
     below Day 2. A subtle bottoming structure in a downtrend.

9. DOWNSIDE GAP TWO RABBITS (bullish)
   - Downtrend; long black candle; a short white candle GAPS DOWN; a second
     white candle opens at/below the prior open and closes above the prior
     close (but still below Day 1's close). The two white candles are the
     "rabbits ready to jump out of their burrow."

10. THREE STARS IN THE SOUTH (bullish)
    - Three black candles with successively LOWER closes but HIGHER lows -
      the downtrend is visibly decaying. Day 1 has a long lower shadow and
      almost no upper shadow; the final day is a small black marubozu.

11. CONCEALING BABY SWALLOW (bullish)
    - Four black candles: two falling black marubozus, a short black candle
      that gaps down but trades up into the prior body (long upper shadow),
      then a fourth black candle that completely engulfs the third including
      its shadow. Despite the new low, the downtrend has eroded.

12. LADDER BOTTOM (bullish, 5 candles)
    - Three strong black candles (like three black crows), a fourth black
      candle with a long upper shadow (first sign of buying), then a strong
      white candle opening above the prior day's body.

13. AFTER BOTTOM GAP UP (bullish, 5 candles)
    - Three black candles stair-stepping down (the third gapping down), a
      white Day 4 covering the gap, and a strong white Day 5 gapping up.
      The color change plus the gap up is the reversal signal.

14. DESCENT BLOCK / DELIBERATION BLOCK (bullish counterparts of bearish
    blocks)
    - Descent Block: three black candles with lower closes, but bodies
      shrinking and lower shadows lengthening - the decline is losing force.
    - Deliberation Block: two normal/long black candles then a short black
      candle or doji gapping down - the final hesitant candle betrays
      seller fatigue.

15. SQUEEZE ALERT (bullish, 3 candles)
    - A black candle followed by two candles with LOWER HIGHS and HIGHER
      LOWS (a contracting triangle in three bars). Colors don't matter.
      Especially prone to upside breaks after a strong down-move.

16. ADVANCE BLOCK (bearish, 3 candles)
    - Three white candles in an uptrend with progressively SHORTER bodies
      and progressively TALLER upper shadows; each opens inside the prior
      body. The rally is running out of fuel. Confirmed when a falling
      candle takes out the midpoint of the FIRST candle's body.

17. DELIBERATION / STALLED PATTERN (bearish, 3 candles)
    - Two long white candles, then a third with a SHORT body (or doji) with
      a tall upper shadow opening near the second candle's close. Note the
      book's stats: it resolved UP 77% of the time - i.e., historically it
      "stalls" more than it reverses. Demand confirmation (a drop through
      the second candle's body midpoint) before shorting.

18. MAT HOLD (continuation, 5 candles)
    - Bullish: long white candle; GAP UP to a small black candle; two more
      small-bodied candles drifting down but holding above Day 1's low; then
      a long white candle closing above Day 2's high. Book stats: 78%
      continue up - one of the strongest continuation signals listed.
    - Bearish version mirrors it (78% continue down). Examples: JAS, QTC.

19. RISING THREE METHODS (continuation)
    - Uptrend; long white candle; three small candles (usually black)
      contained within Day 1's range; a fifth long white candle closing
      above Day 1's close. Book stats: 74% continue up. Example: SPA.

20. FALLING THREE METHODS (continuation)
    - The bearish mirror: 71% continue down per the book. Example: DTAC.

21. THREE LINE STRIKE
    - Bearish version (after a downtrend... note: the book's own stats show
      it resolving UP 84% of the time): three black candles stepping down,
      then a giant white candle engulfing all three. Often a bullish
      exhaustion event despite the name.
    - Bullish version: three white candles, then a giant black engulfing
      candle - resolves DOWN 65% per the book. Lesson: the "strike" candle
      frequently signals the trend is flipping, not continuing.

PART 5 - BROADER STRUCTURE PATTERNS (BOOK SUMMARY)
================================================================================

1. DUMPLING TOP
   - An uptrend that rounds over into a sideways drift of small candles, then
     GAPS DOWN into a downtrend. Rare; the gap down is the key confirmation.
   - Book's example: BTS.

2. FRY PAN BOTTOM
   - The mirror image: a downtrend rounds out through a quiet sideways
     "frying pan," then GAPS UP into a new uptrend.
   - Book's example: BLA.

3. TOWER TOP
   - A long white candle, a cluster of spinning tops (indecision), then a
     long black candle - the "towers" flank a sagging middle. Book stats:
     67% resolve down. Example: MDX.

4. TOWER BOTTOM
   - Long black candle, spinning-top cluster, long white candle. 67% up.
     Example: SMPC.

5. LOW PRICE GAPPING PLAY (bearish continuation)
   - Downtrend; long black candle; up to eleven small spinning tops hovering
     near the low; then a black candle GAPS DOWN. 69% continue down.
   - Example: AP.

6. HIGH PRICE GAPPING PLAY (bullish continuation)
   - The mirror: consolidation near the high of a long white candle, then a
     gap up. 69% continue up.

7. 8-10-12-13 NEW PRICE LINES
   - Eight (or more) consecutive candles each printing a HIGHER HIGH in an
     uptrend. Check the final candle's close: closing over the top of the
     prior real body favors more upside; closing below it warns of a fall.
   - Example: FTSE.

8. SCOOP PATTERN
   - Sideways indecision, a price dip (weak hands selling), then a recovery.
     If price rises back above the indecision zone, a new uptrend begins;
     if not, the pattern fails. Shaped like a scoop/ladle.

9. J-HOOK / INVERTED J-HOOK (continuation)
   - J-Hook: rapid rally (1), bearish signal and pullback (2), indecision
     basing (3), then price re-approaches the prior high - clearing it
     launches a fresh leg up (4). Failure at the high creates a Double Top
     instead. Examples: KCE, SAPPE.
   - Inverted version mirrors this in downtrends.

10. CRADLE PATTERN (bullish reversal)
    - Downtrend; long black candle; a cradle of indecision candles (dojis,
      spinning tops, hammers); then a long white candle showing the strength
      of the new rise. Example: SMPC.

PART 6 - KEY TAKEAWAYS FROM THE BOOK'S STATISTICS
================================================================================
The book attaches "Common Rank" and "Efficiency Rank" plus UP/DOWN percentages
to many patterns. Synthesizing them:

STRONGEST signals it lists (by % agreement):
  - Mat Hold (78% continuation)
  - Deliberation/Stalled (77% - but UP, i.e., it usually does NOT reverse)
  - Rising Three Methods (74%) / Falling Three Methods (71%)
  - Abandoned Baby (70% reversal)
  - Bullish Separating Lines (72%)
  - High/Low Price Gapping Plays (69%)
  - Tower Top/Bottom (67%)
  - Three Line Strike (84% - but in the OPPOSITE direction of its name)

WEAKEST / coin-flip signals it lists:
  - Tri-Star Doji (52/48)
  - Meeting Lines (~50-56)
  - Above/Below the Stomach (40-66 range)
  - Stick Sandwich (38/62 against the "bullish" label)

PRACTICAL LESSON: never trade a pattern in isolation. Even the book's own
data shows several famous "reversal" patterns statistically resolve the other
way. Patterns are meaningful only WHERE they appear (support, resistance,
trendline, FVG) and only WITH confirmation.

PART 7 - ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES & MODERN RESEARCH (ADDED FOR YOUR STUDY)
================================================================================
The book uses 2015-2016 Thai stocks (SET). To strengthen your research, study
the same patterns on these widely available, well-documented charts (pull them
up on TradingView and verify each one yourself - self-verification is the
real training):

1. HAMMER AT MAJOR LOWS
   - S&P 500, March 2020 COVID low (weekly chart): the week of 23 March 2020
     printed a huge lower wick after a waterfall decline - a textbook
     hammer-style rejection that preceded a multi-year rally.
   - Bitcoin, weekly chart around the 2018 bear-market floor (~$3,100-3,200,
     December 2018): long lower wicks and a tweezers-style double rejection
     before the 2019 recovery.

2. BEARISH ENGULFING / DARK CLOUD AT TOPS
   - Nasdaq 100, November 2021 (daily/weekly): repeated upper-wick rejections
     and engulfing candles near the all-time high preceded the 2022 bear
     market.
   - Crude oil (WTI), March 2022 spike above $120: massive upper shadows and
     engulfing reversals as the parabolic move exhausted.

3. MORNING STAR
   - EUR/USD daily around late September 2022 (the ~0.95 low): a cluster of
     small-bodied indecision candles between two large opposite-color
     candles marked the multi-decade low before a 6-month rally.

4. THREE WHITE SOLDIERS
   - Look at strong earnings-gap recoveries in large caps (e.g., mega-cap
     tech in January 2023 daily charts): three consecutive full-bodied green
     candles out of a basing range - exactly the "decline + consolidation +
     soldiers" sequence the book describes.

5. THREE BLACK CROWS
   - Study bank stocks in early March 2023 (US regional banking stress):
     consecutive long red candles opening near prior closes and closing at
     new lows - crows in action before further breakdown.

6. KICKER
   - Any major earnings surprise produces kickers. Example type: a stock in
     a downtrend that gaps above the prior day's high on results and never
     looks back. Build a list from recent earnings seasons and measure
     follow-through over the next 5-10 sessions.

7. MARUBOZU + FOLLOW-THROUGH
   - Index breakout days (e.g., days major indices exit multi-week ranges)
     frequently print near-marubozu candles; check the book's claim that
     early follow-through predicts several more sessions of trend.

RESEARCH TIPS TO STRENGTHEN THE STUDY:
  - Backtest, don't trust: pick ONE pattern, mark the last 50 occurrences on
    a liquid instrument, and record: context (trend), location (S/R? FVG?),
    confirmation (yes/no), and outcome after 3, 5, 10 bars.
  - Academic literature is mixed on candlesticks used ALONE; profitability
    improves materially when patterns are filtered by location (support/
    resistance) and trend context. This matches the book's own validity
    conditions ("the stock must have been in a definite downtrend/uptrend").
  - Higher timeframes (daily, weekly) produce cleaner, less noisy patterns
    than 1-5 minute charts, where spreads and noise degrade the signal.

PART 8 - FAIR VALUE GAPS (FVG)
================================================================================
The book does not cover FVGs; here is the concept and how it links to candles.

DEFINITION:
A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle structure where the middle candle is so
large and fast that the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do NOT overlap. The
untraded void between candle 1's high and candle 3's low (bullish FVG), or
candle 1's low and candle 3's high (bearish FVG), is the "gap."

- BULLISH FVG: created by an aggressive up-move. The gap sits BELOW current
  price and often acts as a magnet/support when price retraces into it.
- BEARISH FVG: created by an aggressive down-move. The gap sits ABOVE price
  and often acts as resistance on a retracement.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Fast moves mean one side overwhelmed the other so quickly that price was
never "fairly" auctioned in that zone. Markets frequently return to these
inefficiencies to fill orders left behind, then continue in the original
direction. Traders treat the FVG as a high-probability reaction zone.

HOW TO TRADE IT:
1. Identify a strong impulsive leg (often contains a marubozu-type candle).
2. Mark the FVG zone from the 3-candle structure.
3. Wait for price to RETRACE into the zone (many use the midpoint of the
   gap, the "consequent encroachment," as the ideal entry area).
4. Demand a candlestick trigger INSIDE the zone: hammer, bullish engulfing,
   morning star (for bullish FVGs); shooting star, bearish engulfing,
   evening star (for bearish FVGs).
5. Stop-loss beyond the far side of the gap; target the origin high/low or
   the next liquidity pool.

KEY NUANCES:
- FVGs in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend are far more reliable
  than counter-trend gaps.
- A gap that is fully traded through and closed beyond usually flips roles
  (a failed bullish FVG becomes resistance) - similar to broken support
  becoming resistance.
- Fresh (untested) gaps outperform gaps that have been tapped repeatedly.

PART 9 - SUPPORT & RESISTANCE (S/R)
================================================================================
DEFINITION:
- SUPPORT: a price zone where buying demand has repeatedly halted declines.
- RESISTANCE: a zone where supply has repeatedly capped rallies.
Think ZONES, not exact lines - draw a band covering the cluster of wicks and
closes, not a single pixel-perfect price.

HOW LEVELS FORM:
- Prior swing highs/lows (the most basic and most watched).
- Round psychological numbers (100, 1.1000 on EUR/USD, 50,000 on BTC).
- High-volume consolidation areas (price "accepted" there before).
- Gap edges and FVG boundaries.
- Moving averages on higher timeframes (dynamic S/R).

CORE PRINCIPLES:
1. ROLE REVERSAL (flip): broken support becomes resistance and vice versa.
   The retest of a broken level is one of the highest-quality trade
   locations in all of technical analysis.
2. TOUCH COUNT: 2-3 clean rejections make a level credible; but a level
   tested MANY times in quick succession is being eroded (each test consumes
   the resting orders) and often breaks.
3. CONFLUENCE: a level gains power when it coincides with a trendline, an
   FVG, a Fibonacci retracement, or a higher-timeframe level.

HOW THE BOOK'S PATTERNS PLUG IN:
The book itself hints at this: it says to pay particular attention to a Dark
Cloud Cover "if it occurs at an important resistance area." Generalize that
rule: EVERY reversal candle in Part 2-4 is graded by its location.
- Hammer AT SUPPORT = A-grade. Hammer in the middle of nowhere = noise.
- Shooting star AT RESISTANCE after an extended rally = A-grade short setup.
- Tweezers bottom sitting exactly ON a prior swing low = double rejection of
  a known demand zone.

PART 10 - TRENDLINES
================================================================================
DEFINITION & DRAWING RULES:
- UPTREND LINE: connect two (validated by a third touch) HIGHER LOWS,
  extended right. It slopes up and acts as dynamic support.
- DOWNTREND LINE: connect LOWER HIGHS; slopes down; dynamic resistance.
- Draw on wicks OR bodies - just be consistent. Two touches suggest a line;
  the third touch validates it; every later touch is tradeable.

USES:
1. TREND FILTER: the book repeats "the stock must have been in a definite
   uptrend/downtrend" for almost every pattern. A trendline is the simplest
   objective way to define that. Above a rising trendline = only take
   bullish reversal patterns at pullbacks; below a falling line = favor
   bearish patterns on rallies.
2. ENTRY LOCATION: the 3rd/4th/5th touch of a valid trendline is a location
   to hunt for a trigger candle (hammer on an uptrend-line touch; shooting
   star on a downtrend-line touch).
3. BREAK + RETEST: a decisive close through a mature trendline, followed by
   a retest of the line from the other side, is a classic trend-change
   entry - and the retest candle is very often one of the book's patterns
   (engulfing, piercing, morning star).

WARNINGS:
- Steep trendlines (>45-60 degrees) break quickly; the break often just
  means the trend is slowing, not reversing.
- Constantly redrawing a line to fit price means there is no line. Respect
  only lines the market has respected.

PART 11 - THE PLAYBOOK: PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER IN REAL LIFE
================================================================================
The professional sequence is TOP-DOWN and always the same:

  CONTEXT (trend)  ->  LOCATION (S/R, trendline, FVG)  ->  TRIGGER (candle)
  ->  CONFIRMATION  ->  RISK-DEFINED ENTRY  ->  MANAGEMENT

STEP 1: HIGHER-TIMEFRAME CONTEXT (Weekly/Daily)
  - Is the market in an uptrend (higher highs + higher lows / above a rising
    trendline), a downtrend, or a range?
  - Decision rule: trade WITH the higher-timeframe trend by default.
    Counter-trend reversal patterns need extra confluence (major S/R plus
    exhaustion signals like gravestone doji, three black crows, or an
    abandoned baby).

STEP 2: MAP LOCATIONS ON THE TRADING TIMEFRAME (Daily/4H/1H)
  - Mark horizontal S/R zones (swing highs/lows, flip zones).
  - Draw the active trendlines.
  - Mark fresh FVGs from recent impulsive legs.
  - CONFLUENCE ZONES = where two or more of these overlap. These are your
    ONLY hunting grounds. If price is not at a zone, you have no trade -
    you wait.

STEP 3: WAIT FOR THE TRIGGER CANDLE AT THE ZONE
  Bullish zone (support + rising trendline + bullish FVG):
    - Hammer / dragonfly doji (rejection)
    - Bullish engulfing / piercing line (aggression)
    - Morning star / tweezers bottom / bullish harami (transition)
  Bearish zone (resistance + falling trendline + bearish FVG):
    - Shooting star / gravestone doji
    - Bearish engulfing / dark cloud cover
    - Evening star / tweezers top / hanging man

STEP 4: CONFIRMATION (the book insists on this - honor it)
  - The book's validity rules repeatedly require the NEXT day to continue in
    the signal's direction (continued buying after a hammer; continued
    selling after a hanging man / shooting star).
  - Practical options:
    a) Conservative: wait for the next candle to close beyond the trigger
       candle's high (long) or low (short), OR
    b) Aggressive: enter on the break of the trigger candle's extreme with a
       stop beyond its opposite extreme.
  - Volume adds conviction: reversal candles on above-average volume (the
    book highlights this for morning stars, dark cloud covers, and homing
    pigeons) carry more weight.

STEP 5: ENTRY, STOP, TARGET
  - ENTRY: at confirmation (per Step 4) or on a retrace to the trigger
    candle's midpoint / the FVG midpoint for better R:R.
  - STOP-LOSS: beyond the structural invalidation point, NOT an arbitrary
    pip count:
      * Long at support: below the zone / below the hammer's low / below
        the FVG's lower boundary.
      * Short at resistance: above the zone / above the shooting star's
        high / above the FVG's upper boundary.
  - TARGETS:
      * T1 = nearest opposing S/R level or opposing FVG (take partials).
      * T2 = the origin of the prior impulse / the next major zone.
  - Only take trades offering at least 1:2 risk-to-reward; skip anything
    less regardless of how pretty the candle looks.

STEP 6: MANAGE
  - After T1, move the stop to breakeven or trail it under successive
    higher lows (using your trendline as the trailing guide).
  - If the confirmation candle FAILS (e.g., the day after a hammer closes
    below the hammer's low), exit immediately - the pattern is dead, and
    per the book's own statistics, failed patterns often run hard the
    other way (see Three Line Strike).

WORKED EXAMPLE SCENARIOS (TEMPLATES TO REHEARSE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENARIO A - Trend-continuation long (highest probability template):
  1. Daily chart in an uptrend, above a rising trendline.
  2. Price pulls back into a confluence zone: old resistance-turned-support
     + a fresh bullish FVG + the 3rd touch of the trendline.
  3. Trigger: hammer forms inside the FVG; next day opens and pushes above
     the hammer's high (confirmation = "continued buying").
  4. Entry on the break; stop below the hammer's low AND the FVG; target
     the prior swing high (T1) and a measured extension (T2).
  This is precisely the book's hammer checklist, upgraded with location.

SCENARIO B - Reversal short at a major top:
  1. Extended uptrend (8+ new price lines - the book's own exhaustion count)
     into a weekly resistance zone.
  2. Bearish trigger cluster: shooting star, then a bearish engulfing or
     dark cloud cover on strong volume AT the zone (the book explicitly
     upgrades dark cloud at "an important resistance area" with "strong
     end-of-day volume").
  3. Confirmation: next candle closes below the cluster's midpoint.
  4. Entry short; stop above the pattern high; T1 = rising trendline below;
     T2 = the last bullish FVG (gaps get filled).

SCENARIO C - Break-and-retest trend change:
  1. Mature uptrend line breaks with a decisive marubozu-type close below.
  2. Price rallies back to retest the broken line, which now coincides with
     a bearish FVG created by the breakdown leg.
  3. Trigger at the retest: evening star or bearish harami.
  4. Short with stop above the retest high; target the next support shelf.

SCENARIO D - Range trading:
  1. Clear horizontal range (Tower/Dumpling-style consolidations qualify).
  2. Buy only bullish triggers (tweezers bottom, piercing) at range support;
     sell only bearish triggers at range resistance.
  3. Abandon the range plan the moment a gapping play (High/Low Price
     Gapping Play) or a breakout marubozu appears - those are the book's
     continuation signals telling you the range is resolving.

PART 12 - RISK MANAGEMENT & PRE-TRADE CHECKLIST
================================================================================
RISK RULES (non-negotiable):
  - Risk a fixed fraction per trade: 0.5%-2% of account equity. At 1% risk,
    a 10-trade losing streak = ~10% drawdown: survivable. At 10% risk, the
    same streak is ruin.
  - Position size = (Account x Risk%) / (Entry - Stop distance).
  - Never widen a stop. Never average into a loser.
  - Cap total open risk (e.g., max 3 concurrent positions / 3% aggregate).
  - Journal EVERY trade: screenshot at entry, the pattern, the location, the
    confirmation, and the outcome. Your journal becomes your personal
    version of the book's UP%/DOWN% statistics - on the markets YOU trade.

PRE-TRADE CHECKLIST (print this):
  [ ] What is the higher-timeframe trend? Am I with it or against it?
  [ ] Is price AT a marked zone (S/R, trendline touch, FVG)? If not - NO TRADE.
  [ ] Is there a valid trigger candle from the playbook?
  [ ] Does the pattern meet the book's validity conditions (prior trend,
      shadow-to-body ratios, gap requirements)?
  [ ] Do I have confirmation (follow-through candle / volume)?
  [ ] Is R:R at least 1:2 to the first logical target?
  [ ] Where EXACTLY is my invalidation (stop)? Position sized to it?
  [ ] Any news/earnings imminent that could gap through my stop?
  [ ] Am I emotionally neutral (not revenge trading, not FOMO)?

PART 13 - COMMON MISTAKES & FINAL NOTES
================================================================================
MISTAKES THE BOOK'S OWN DATA WARNS ABOUT:
  1. Trading pattern NAMES instead of statistics - e.g., the "Bearish" Three
     Line Strike resolved UP 84% of the time, and the "bearish" Deliberation
     pattern resolved UP 77% of the time. Verify; don't assume.
  2. Ignoring the prior-trend requirement. A hammer without a preceding
     downtrend is just a candle with a wick.
  3. Skipping confirmation. Nearly every pattern description in the book
     says "confirmation is required by the candles that follow."
  4. Trading indecision candles (dojis, spinning tops, high waves) as
     signals. The book is explicit: they mean JOIN THE SIDELINES and wait.

MISTAKES FROM THE BROADER TOOLKIT:
  5. Taking triggers in the middle of nowhere (no zone = no trade).
  6. Treating S/R as exact lines and getting wicked out - use zones and
     place stops beyond the ZONE, not the line.
  7. Chasing filled FVGs or 10th-touch trendlines - freshness matters.
  8. Over-leveraging a "high probability" setup. 78% (Mat Hold) still fails
     more than 1 in 5 times.

FINAL SYNTHESIS:
Candlestick patterns are the LANGUAGE of the chart - they tell you what
buyers and sellers just did. Support/resistance, trendlines, and FVGs are the
MAP - they tell you where that language matters. Confirmation and risk
management are the LAW - they keep you solvent while your statistical edge
plays out. The book gives you the vocabulary and validity rules; this
playbook gives you the grammar. Rehearse the four scenario templates on
historical charts until spotting them is reflexive, journal your live
results, and let your own data - not memorized pattern names - drive which
setups you keep trading.
